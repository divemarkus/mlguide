# Garmin Descent Mk3i

The **Descent Mk3i 43 mm (010-02753-13)** is considerably more capable than a “dive watch.” I would treat it as a **multisport instrumentation platform that happens to contain a serious dive computer**.

Garmin lists **12 sensor systems** for the Mk3i: GPS/GLONASS/Galileo + SatIQ, Elevate wrist HR, Pulse Ox, barometric altimeter, compass, gyroscope, accelerometer, thermometer, ambient-light sensor, and depth sensor. ([Garmin][1])

# Your Mk3i sensor stack

| Sensor                            | What it actually measures                   | Daily-life use                                       | Diving use                                                                           | My recommendation     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------- |
| **GNSS: GPS / GLONASS / Galileo** | Position, speed, track, time                | Walking, hiking, running, travel, navigation         | Surface position/entry-exit tracking                                                 | **Use constantly**    |
| **SatIQ / multi-band GNSS**       | Dynamically optimized satellite positioning | Urban areas, mountains, difficult GPS environments   | Surface navigation                                                                   | **Enable Auto/SatIQ** |
| **Garmin Elevate HR**             | Optical heart rate                          | Resting HR, workouts, stress, recovery, sleep        | HR during dives + physiological tracking                                             | **24/7**              |
| **Pulse Ox**                      | Blood oxygen saturation                     | Baseline/trend, altitude, sleep-related observations | Useful supplemental information around altitude/travel; not a dive-safety substitute | **Selective**         |
| **Barometric altimeter**          | Atmospheric pressure → elevation            | Hiking, stairs, elevation gain, weather              | Altitude diving/decompression calculations                                           | **Always on**         |
| **Compass**                       | Magnetic heading                            | Hiking, navigation, orientation                      | Underwater navigation                                                                | **Learn to use it**   |
| **Gyroscope**                     | Rotation/angular movement                   | Activity recognition, motion/orientation             | Dive motion/orientation                                                              | **Automatic**         |
| **Accelerometer**                 | Linear movement                             | Steps, movement, reps, activity detection            | Movement/depth/activity algorithms                                                   | **Automatic**         |
| **Thermometer**                   | Temperature                                 | Environmental/trend data                             | Water/environmental temperature                                                      | **Use deliberately**  |
| **Ambient-light sensor**          | Surrounding light                           | AMOLED brightness/power behavior                     | Low-light display management                                                         | **Automatic**         |
| **Depth sensor**                  | Water pressure/depth                        | Essentially dormant                                  | **Actual dive computer**                                                             | **Dive use**          |

The really interesting part is that Garmin doesn't simply expose these sensors individually. It **fuses them into higher-level measurements**—Body Battery, stress, respiration, sleep, recovery, elevation, navigation, training load, dive decompression, etc. ([Garmin][2])

---

# 1. Garmin Elevate wrist HR sensor

This is probably the **most useful sensor for daily life**.

Garmin specifies continuous heart-rate monitoring, resting HR and high/low abnormal-HR alerts. ([Garmin][1])

### What you can do with it

**24/7**

* Resting heart rate
* HR trends
* Stress
* Body Battery
* Recovery
* Sleep
* Respiration
* Training load
* Training readiness
* Calories

### During exercise

Use it for:

* cycling
* running
* walking
* strength training
* rowing
* swimming
* hiking
* indoor cardio

And because you're an experienced athlete, I'd go one step further:

### Create HR zones

Set your HR zones based on your actual physiology rather than simply accepting Garmin's defaults.

Then you can distinguish:

**Zone 1:** recovery
**Zone 2:** aerobic base
**Zone 3:** moderate
**Zone 4:** threshold
**Zone 5:** maximal effort

That makes the Mk3i much more useful as a training instrument.

### One upgrade I'd consider

For hard workouts, cycling or serious training, pair a **Garmin HRM-Pro Plus**.

The watch's optical HR is excellent for everyday monitoring, but a chest strap gives you a cleaner signal during high-intensity and rapidly changing HR conditions.

---

# 2. Pulse Ox

This is the red-light optical sensor on the back.

Garmin allows both spot measurements and longer-term Pulse Ox tracking. A manual reading takes up to ~30 seconds while you remain still. ([Garmin][3])

### Useful applications

I'd use Pulse Ox **selectively rather than continuously**, because continuous Pulse Ox consumes battery.

Good use cases:

### Baseline

Take a reading:

* sitting quietly
* at approximately the same time
* under similar conditions

You're looking for **your normal baseline and trends**, not obsessing over individual readings.

### Altitude

This becomes much more interesting when hiking/skiing/mountaineering.

Garmin combines Pulse Ox with altitude information for altitude acclimation. Above 800 m / 2,625 ft, the watch can track altitude-adjusted Pulse Ox, respiration and resting HR trends. ([Garmin][2])

### Travel

It can also be interesting when you're traveling to high altitude.

### Diving

I'd regard Pulse Ox as **supplemental physiological information**, not something to use to make dive/no-dive decisions.

---

# 3. Barometric altimeter

This is a **very useful sensor that most smartwatch owners underutilize**.

It measures atmospheric pressure and converts that into elevation. Garmin says it operates continuously, including low-power mode. ([Garmin][4])

### Daily uses

You can use it for:

* hiking elevation
* stair climbing
* mountain biking
* skiing
* trail running
* elevation gain/loss
* altitude acclimatization
* weather trends

### A particularly useful trick

Suppose you're sitting at home.

Atmospheric pressure changes:

**Pressure falling → potentially deteriorating weather**

**Pressure rising → potentially improving weather**

The Mk3i can therefore act as a miniature **barometric weather instrument**.

### Calibration

Garmin lets you calibrate it using:

* known elevation
* DEM
* GPS
* automatic GPS calibration

([Garmin][5])

For serious hiking/mountain use, I'd keep **Auto Cal.** enabled.

---

# 4. Compass

The Mk3i has a real electronic compass.

This is much more useful than simply having a compass widget.

### Daily

Use it for:

* hiking
* trail navigation
* finding bearings
* orienting maps
* navigation when GPS isn't ideal

### Diving

This is where it becomes particularly interesting for you.

You can use it for:

**Underwater reciprocal navigation**

Example:

> Descend → heading 120° → swim 8 minutes → turn 180° → return on 300°.

You can also use it for:

* reef navigation
* wreck navigation
* locating entry/exit direction
* underwater photography routes

Obviously, underwater compass navigation remains subject to normal diving limitations, and the watch shouldn't replace your normal navigation/bearing procedures.

---

# 5. Gyroscope

This is one of the sensors people rarely think about.

The gyro measures **rotational movement**.

Think:

> "How is my wrist/watch rotating?"

It contributes to Garmin's understanding of:

* movement
* orientation
* activity
* exercise
* navigation
* swimming
* diving

### What you need to do

Basically:

**Nothing.**

The gyro is one of those sensors where the best strategy is to let Garmin's algorithms exploit it.

But you *are* benefiting from it whenever the watch recognizes movement and activities.

---

# 6. Accelerometer

This detects **linear acceleration and movement**.

It is fundamental to:

* steps
* activity detection
* movement
* sleep detection
* exercise recognition
* cadence-related measurements
* gesture interactions
* various fitness calculations

### Your best use

Wear the Mk3i essentially **24/7**.

The longer Garmin sees your normal movement patterns, the more useful its physiological models become.

---

# 7. Thermometer

This one is interesting.

The Mk3i contains an actual thermometer sensor. ([Garmin][1])

But there's an important limitation:

**Your wrist temperature is not necessarily ambient temperature.**

Your skin and body heat influence it.

### Daily use

It's therefore better regarded as:

**environmental/trend sensor**

rather than:

> "What's the exact outdoor temperature?"

### Diving

This gets much more interesting.

The water environment is obviously very different from your wrist's normal environment.

You can use temperature information alongside:

* depth
* dive duration
* surface interval
* gas consumption
* exposure protection

for a much better understanding of your dives.

---

# 8. Ambient-light sensor

This is the least interesting sensor from a data perspective—but it's still useful.

It detects surrounding light and helps the AMOLED display behave appropriately.

So:

**bright sunlight → display needs more illumination**

**dark room → less illumination**

It helps balance:

**visibility ↔ battery consumption**

You don't really need to interact with it.

---

# 9. Depth sensor

Now we get to the **big one**.

The Mk3i has a dedicated depth sensor rated to **200 m / 656 ft**, with 0.1 m resolution down to 99.9 m and 1 m resolution from 100–200 m. ([Garmin][6])

This isn't merely a waterproof smartwatch.

It is a **real dive computer**.

### It measures

* current depth
* maximum depth
* ascent/descent
* dive duration
* decompression information

And feeds that into the dive algorithms.

The Mk3 series supports:

* Single Gas
* Multi-Gas
* CCR
* Gauge
* Apnea
* Apnea Hunt
* Pool Apnea

([Garmin][7])

For someone with your diving background, this is the feature set I'd spend the most time learning.

---

# 10. GPS / GLONASS / Galileo

The Mk3i has:

**GPS + GLONASS + Galileo**

and Garmin's **SatIQ** technology. ([Garmin][1])

SatIQ dynamically determines the appropriate satellite mode to balance:

**accuracy ↔ battery life**

This is particularly useful because you don't want maximum-power multi-band GNSS running unnecessarily.

### Your practical setup

I'd use:

**SatIQ / Auto**

for normal activities.

Use higher-accuracy multi-band GNSS when doing:

* mountain hiking
* difficult urban navigation
* narrow valleys
* trail running
* precise tracks

Garmin specifically describes multi-band GPS and SatIQ as navigation features of the Mk3 series. ([Garmin][8])

# The really cool part: sensor fusion

This is where the Mk3i becomes much more interesting.

Consider a hike.

The watch simultaneously knows:

**GPS**
→ where you are

**Accelerometer**
→ you're moving

**Gyroscope**
→ how you're moving/oriented

**Compass**
→ direction

**Barometer**
→ elevation/pressure

**HR**
→ physiological effort

**Pulse Ox**
→ oxygen saturation

**Thermometer**
→ environmental/temperature information

Garmin can then derive:

> "You're at 1,200 m, climbed 600 m, walked 8.2 km, averaged 128 BPM, spent 72 minutes in aerobic zones, your Body Battery dropped X, and your recovery requirement is Y."

That's vastly more useful than looking at each sensor independently.

---

# And there's one more sensor you didn't mention: SubWave

Your particular **Mk3i**, unlike the standard Mk3, supports Garmin's **SubWave** system.

This is what makes the **T2 transmitter** capability possible.

The Mk3i can pair with Descent T2 transceivers for:

* tank pressure
* gas consumption
* air integration
* diver-to-diver messaging

Garmin specifically identifies air integration and diver messaging as Mk3i differentiators. ([Garmin][9])

If you eventually buy a T2, the watch becomes substantially more interesting as a dive computer.

# One thing I would definitely do

Since you've owned this watch but haven't actually used its dive capabilities:

### Do a "sensor commissioning" exercise.

Not a dive yet.

Spend an afternoon systematically testing:

**1. HR**
Measure resting HR.

**2. Pulse Ox**
Take three stationary measurements.

**3. Compass**
Compare against a known compass.

**4. Altimeter**
Compare against a known elevation.

**5. Barometer**
Record pressure and watch it over several hours.

**6. Thermometer**
Compare against an independent thermometer—but understand wrist/environmental temperature limitations.

**7. GPS**
Walk a known route and inspect the track.

**8. Accelerometer**
Walk 1,000 steps and compare.

**9. Gyroscope**
Nothing special required—verify orientation/motion features.

**10. Depth sensor**
Don't try to "test" this outside water. Save it for an actual controlled dive/pool environment.

That gives you a **baseline for every physical sensor in the watch**.

# Your Mk3i's hidden superpower

Given your interests, I wouldn't primarily think of the Mk3i as a smartwatch.

I'd think of it as:

> **a wearable sensor platform + navigation computer + physiological monitor + dive computer.**

And the sensor architecture is actually very good:

**GNSS**
↓
Position / navigation

**Accelerometer + gyroscope**
↓
Movement / activity

**Compass**
↓
Heading

**Barometer**
↓
Elevation + atmospheric pressure

**Thermometer**
↓
Temperature

**Elevate HR + Pulse Ox**
↓
Physiology

**Depth sensor**
↓
Dive computer

**Sensor fusion + Garmin algorithms**
↓
**Training / recovery / navigation / diving / health metrics**

And because your Mk3i is the **43 mm AMOLED model**, you also have the advantage of a very compact 43 × 43 × 14.3 mm package, sapphire crystal, 32 GB storage, and up to 10 days of smartwatch battery life (less with always-on display). ([Garmin][11])

### If I were setting yours up from scratch

I'd build you a **"Mk3i Maximum Utilization" configuration** next: exact **Glances, Controls, Sensors & Accessories, Activity profiles, data screens, dive screens, GPS settings, Pulse Ox settings, HR settings, battery settings, and Garmin Connect configuration**—essentially a checklist to turn the watch into a fully utilized daily + training + outdoor + scuba instrument.
