# AR/VR Glasses

Yes. The industry is at an interesting inflection point in **2026**. The biggest mistake is to think of "AR/VR glasses" as one market. It is really **four converging markets**:

1. **AI smart glasses** — camera/audio/AI, often no display
2. **Display AR glasses** — XREAL/VITURE/RayNeo type
3. **Spatial-computing glasses** — Aura/Android XR type
4. **Immersive VR/MR headsets** — Quest/Vision Pro/SteamVR type

And the industry is increasingly moving **from #4 toward #1–3** for everyday computing.

IDC says display-less smart glasses shipped about **2.25 million units in Q1 2026**, up **167% YoY**, while the broader eyewear AR/VR segment grew 86%. Meta had **69.2% of smart-glasses shipments** in IDC's Q1 data. ([IDC][1])

## The big picture

I would visualize the industry like this:

```text
                    FUTURE COMPUTING
                           │
             ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
             │                           │
        EVERYDAY WEAR                IMMERSIVE
             │                           │
       AI / AR GLASSES              VR / MR
             │                           │
    ┌────────┼────────┐          ┌───────┼────────┐
    │        │        │          │       │        │
   Meta    Google   XREAL      Meta    Apple    Valve
           Android             Quest   Vision   SteamVR
             XR
```

The **everyday glasses side is growing much faster** than traditional VR.

# 1. 🥇 Google — Android XR

This is the ecosystem I would watch **most closely**.

Google is trying to do something very similar to what Android did to smartphones:

> **Create the operating system/platform while letting many manufacturers build the hardware.**

Android XR is being developed with **Samsung and Qualcomm**, and Google explicitly designed it for both **headsets and glasses**. It supports Android development tools, Unity and OpenXR. ([blog.google][2])

And this is the critical part:

### Android XR isn't just VR.

Google is building two classes of eyewear:

**Audio glasses**

and

**Display glasses**

Google explicitly describes display glasses as showing information when you need it, while audio glasses provide spoken assistance. ([blog.google][3])

That's exactly where **XREAL Aura** fits.

### Ecosystem:

```text
                    GOOGLE
                       │
                 ANDROID XR
                       │
       ┌───────────────┼────────────────┐
       │               │                │
    Samsung          XREAL           Others
       │               │
   Galaxy XR         Aura
       │               │
       └───────────────┼────────────────┘
                       │
                 Gemini / AI
                       │
       ┌───────────────┼───────────────┐
     Maps           Chrome          Google Play
     YouTube        Photos           Apps
```

This is why I became more interested in Aura during our discussion.

**Aura isn't just an XREAL product. It is an Android XR product.**

That's potentially much more important.

# 2. 🥈 Meta — currently the king of smart glasses

This is the distinction between **current dominance** and **future platform strategy**.

Meta is currently crushing the smart-glasses market.

IDC gives Meta **69.2% Q1 2026 market share**. Counterpoint puts Meta at an even higher **84% share** of the global smart-glasses market, depending on the category definition. ([IDC][4])

And the reason is brilliant:

### They made smart glasses look like normal glasses.

Ray-Ban Meta:

* looks normal
* has cameras
* speakers
* microphones
* AI
* phone integration
* social sharing
* navigation
* translation
* music

That's a much easier consumer proposition than:

> "Put this giant VR computer on your face."

Meta's latest software continues adding capabilities such as navigation, spatial audio and expanded AI functionality. ([Android Central][5])

### Meta's strategic path

```text
Ray-Ban Meta
     ↓
AI glasses
     ↓
display glasses
     ↓
AR glasses
     ↓
true AR
     ↓
eventually lightweight replacement for phone
```

And Meta has **Orion** as its long-term AR research direction.

So I would not underestimate Meta.

# 3. 🥉 Apple — potentially the premium spatial-computing ecosystem

Apple is different.

Apple isn't currently trying to win the cheap glasses market.

Its strength is:

**hardware + OS + developer ecosystem + silicon + services**

Vision Pro/visionOS established Apple's spatial-computing architecture around:

* windows
* volumes
* immersive spaces
* RealityKit
* ARKit
* hand tracking
* eye tracking
* spatial audio

Apple's developer ecosystem is already built around these concepts. ([Apple Developer][6])

The eventual Apple strategy I would watch is:

```text
Vision Pro
     ↓
lighter headset
     ↓
Apple spatial computer
     ↓
Apple glasses
```

Apple's advantage is enormous if it can shrink the technology.

Because it already owns:

**iPhone + Mac + iPad + Apple Watch + AirPods + App Store + Apple silicon + visionOS**

That could become an extremely powerful spatial-computing ecosystem.

# 4. Qualcomm — the company people overlook

Qualcomm isn't necessarily going to sell you glasses.

It may provide the **brains inside everybody else's glasses**.

That's strategically important.

Qualcomm supplies XR silicon to companies including:

* Meta
* Samsung
* XREAL
* other XR manufacturers

And the newer Snapdragon Reality Elite platform is particularly interesting because it combines:

**CPU + GPU + NPU + XR processing + AI**

The Aura puck is an excellent example.

So the future could look like:

```text
Google Android XR
        │
        ▼
Qualcomm silicon
        │
 ┌──────┼─────────┐
 ▼      ▼         ▼
Samsung XREAL   other OEMs
```

That's extremely similar to the traditional Android/Qualcomm smartphone ecosystem.

# 5. XREAL — probably the most interesting independent glasses company

XREAL is particularly interesting because it has been focused almost entirely on **glasses rather than giant VR headsets**.

Its evolution is roughly:

```text
XREAL Air
    ↓
XREAL One
    ↓
One Pro
    ↓
AURA
    ↓
Android XR
```

Aura represents a major strategic shift.

XREAL is no longer saying:

> "We make a display you wear."

It's saying:

> **"We make a spatial computer you wear."**

Aura has:

* Android XR
* Gemini
* 70° FOV
* 6DoF
* cameras
* hand tracking
* Google Play
* external compute puck
* Snapdragon Reality Elite

XREAL says millions of Google Play apps will be available on Aura, alongside 100+ XR-specific applications. ([XREAL][7])

That puts XREAL in a potentially excellent position **if Android XR wins**.

# 6. Samsung — sleeper giant

Samsung shouldn't be overlooked.

Samsung has:

**phones + displays + memory + cameras + wearables + TVs + XR**

and is Google's primary hardware partner for Android XR.

Samsung's first Android XR headset was the foundation for the ecosystem, while Google is extending Android XR toward glasses. ([blog.google][2])

Samsung's advantage is manufacturing scale.

If Android XR becomes the Android equivalent of visionOS, Samsung could become:

> **the Samsung Galaxy of spatial computing.**

# And then there's the "open" ecosystem

This is something I think **you specifically should care about**.

I'd watch:

### OpenXR

more than individual headset brands.

OpenXR allows applications to target a common XR interface rather than building completely separate software for every headset.

Google is supporting OpenXR in Android XR from the beginning. ([blog.google][2])

Valve/SteamVR is deeply tied into that broader PCXR ecosystem.

If OpenXR becomes the:

> **"USB of XR"**

then hardware manufacturers can compete on:

* optics
* cameras
* weight
* compute
* battery
* price

while developers don't have to rewrite everything for each headset.

That would be very good for consumers.

My prediction:

### 2026–2027

**AI glasses explode.**

Mostly:

> camera + microphone + speakers + AI

Meta dominates.

---

### 2027–2029

**Display glasses become mainstream.**

The glasses start providing:

* notifications
* navigation
* translation
* virtual monitors
* AI
* visual search
* contextual information

This is where **Android XR vs Meta** becomes particularly important.

---

### 2029–2032

The boundary between:

**phone + glasses + earbuds + AI assistant**

starts disappearing.

Your phone becomes increasingly:

> **the compute/network/storage companion**

while glasses become:

> **the primary visual interface.**

I don't think the ultimate product is:

> **VR headset**

I think it's closer to:

> **normal-looking glasses with a computer attached invisibly to the ecosystem.**

Something like:

```text
             YOU
              │
      ┌───────┴────────┐
      │                │
    Glasses          Earbuds
      │                │
      └───────┬────────┘
              │
```
